About the data: where it comes from, how much there is, and its limits

Where the text comes from

Every answer is built from the USGS Geologic Names Lexicon (Geolex), the US Geological Survey's register of named rock units. For each unit, Geolex holds short summaries of the published references that defined or revised it, plus curated metadata: its age, the states it occurs in, its geologic province, and how it nests inside larger units. The rock-type and timescale vocabularies come from Macrostrat. The corpus was downloaded by the first lab in this repo (geo-sft) and is reused unchanged.

How the graph was built

Facts come from two sources of very different reliability, and every relationship records which one it came from. The knowledge graph and GraphRAG read these; vector RAG reads only the passages.

An edge asserted by both sources is counted under each. geolex: curated by the USGS. text: extracted from passages by pattern-matching rules. derived: computed (filing, mentions, the timescale).

Limitations

  • Units A to C only. The corpus holds the first alphabetical slice of Geolex (see above). A question about the Eagle Ford, the Morrison or the Navajo Sandstone will find little or nothing, and every count is a count of A–C units, not of all US units.
  • US units only. Geolex covers the United States and its territories.
  • Summaries, not papers. Passages are lexicon summaries of references written between … and … (median …). Much is archaic, and older passages can contradict newer ones: names, ranks and ages were revised over time.
  • Rule-extracted relationships are noisy. "Overlies", "equivalent to" and text-derived rock types come from pattern-matching rules. When the first lab reviewed 150 rows labelled by those rules (a machine review, not a geologist's), 83% had at least one error.
  • Name matching is imperfect. The same name can belong to more than one unit (there are two Adas). Units mentioned in the text but outside the corpus appear as … placeholder units with no curated age, rock type or state.
  • Ages come only from curated metadata, so a unit whose Geolex entry has no parseable age will not match an age query in the graph, even if its passages mention one.
  • No maps, figures or tables: text and metadata only.

Geolex is a US Government work in the public domain. Macrostrat vocabularies are CC-BY 4.0. Counts are measured from the loaded database; the Geolex total (16,684) is from when geo-sft fetched the corpus, and the 83% is from geo-sft's gold review.
